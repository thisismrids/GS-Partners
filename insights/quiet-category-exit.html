The Quiet Category Exit

Why regional FMCG brands are missing from India's fastest-growing shelf, and a five-minute way to check your own exposure.

Quick commerce (Blinkit, Zepto, Instamart) has quietly become a real discovery and repurchase channel for FMCG and personal care in India's metros. For a listed national brand, that's an upside channel. For a regional or legacy brand that isn't listed, or is listed inconsistently, it's a channel where a competitor is winning orders that used to be uncontested.

The uncomfortable part is that this is easy to miss from the inside. Overall revenue can look stable, since general trade and modern trade often decline slowly enough to mask what's actually happening, while the specific, fast-growing slice of demand that runs through quick commerce goes almost entirely to whoever is already listed.

It's a listing problem before it's a marketing problem

The instinct, when a brand notices this, is to reach for a marketing fix: more spend, a new campaign. But if the product isn't reliably in stock and searchable on the apps where the demand actually lives, no amount of marketing spend upstream will convert it. The diligence question comes first: are you even in the room.

Before you diagnose why you're losing, check whether you're actually present.

A five-minute self-check

This is roughly the first pass we run before any category-strategy conversation. It uses only what's publicly visible on the apps themselves:

  1. Open Blinkit, Zepto, and Instamart in your primary city. Search your top three SKUs by name, and separately by category.
  2. Note whether you appear at all, and if you do, whether you're in stock, and how many competitor SKUs are listed around yours.
  3. Repeat the same search in two or three other metros where you distribute. Presence is rarely uniform city to city.
  4. Check whether competitors are running visible in-app promotions or sponsored placement for your category, and whether you are.
  5. If you're absent, thin, or frequently out of stock in two of the three cities you checked, you have a live exposure, not a hypothetical one.

What to do with the answer

If the check comes back clean, you've lost twenty minutes confirming you're fine. If it doesn't, you now have something specific and sized: not "quick commerce is a threat," but which cities, which SKUs, and roughly how much shelf you've already ceded. That's the difference between a strategy conversation and an actual finding.
